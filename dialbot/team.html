THE PEOPLE

The team behind Dial Bot.

Five people shaped the product, the assistant, its Meetings home, and the connections behind it.

Product Designer

Amit

View profile ↗

Amit gave Dial Bot its face and feel. His North Star demo aligned the team, and he mapped the end-to-end journey while designing the Bot’s identity, personality, motion, interactions, brand, and marketing site. He also authored the merged Meetings PR for the synthetic video track and safeguards around camera capture and private replies.

Applied AI Scientist

Yacine

View profile ↗

Yacine built the assistant brain with speaker-scoped, read-only Atlassian and Dialpad connections plus tools for meeting state, timers, chat, and shared-screen questions. His merged meeting work added wake detection, short spoken answers, follow-up turns, private greetings, and late-join catch-up. A connected-account expansion remains draft.

Meetings DRI

Vishal

View profile ↗

Vishal's merged speech work silenced the guest's fake mic, preserved outgoing speech when media was rebuilt, and stopped Bot speech when a person talked over it or muted. A separate open PR proposes host-scoped, read-only Glean access; that addition is still under review.

Meetings specialist engineer

Himanshu

Himanshu proposed HTTP invite and remove endpoints and wired the UC RemoteDialBotHost path in an open backend PR. Its server launch functions are still stubs, so the PR establishes an API seam but does not prove an end-to-end Bot join. The work remains under review.

Product Integrations Engineer

Jimut

Jimut authored local changes to Host Controls visibility, room state, and the beta guest's launch and pairing setup. Those commits sit within a shared draft integration. Its PR body is empty, so this credit follows the inspected commits and does not attribute the connected-source connectors to him.

THE WORK

Specialist sub-agents

AI sub-agents filled roles usually held by people: engineers, designers, writers, producers, and reviewers. Open each role to see the work it helped complete. These work credits are not a count of distinct agents.

Product & engineering

01Meetings integration engineerThe Bot arrives as a silent meeting guest.

Focused local work stabilized host autojoin and kept the guest quiet until addressed. This credit describes the tested local join path, not every final meeting behavior.

02Runtime engineerA safer path for the beta guest to connect.

The local launcher restored file-backed pairing and browser network checks so the guest could connect without placing the pairing value in the page.

03Privacy engineerWho can ask, and where the answer goes.

The integration review mapped speaker-scoped, read-only source access and private replies for connected context. This is a reviewed privacy boundary, not a claim that this assignment implemented the connector architecture.

04Product designerA draft way to choose personal sources.

A separate draft explored Settings setup and an attendee entry prompt for connecting a person’s context. It remains preview work until the real grant and permission flow is integrated.

05Motion engineerThe familiar Dial Bot identity in Meetings.

The approved Mood Kit was translated into reusable tile states for Standby, Listening, Thinking, Speaking, and Paused, keeping the Bot’s matte identity across the meeting view.

06Software engineerOne copyable answer in room chat.

A focused local fix addressed repeated source links after the live review showed the same meeting link twice. This credit is for that narrow chat-quality repair.

07Meetings engineerClearer behavior when a timer is requested privately.

A local change clarified private-chat timer messaging while the shared timer action received a separate room check. It should not be read as proof that every live timer request succeeds.

08Frontend engineerThe film can be watched and revisited by chapter.

The local chapter player gained seeking from its idle frame, captions, quiet controls below the film, a poster, and active chapter context. Player and site work share the same local source, so they appear as one credit.

09Motion designerA restrained visual language for the Bot.

An isolated study developed five base states and three exploratory expressions around the matte Dial Bot contour. It also checked keyboard use and reduced-motion behavior. The extra expressions are design explorations, not shipped meeting behavior.

10Film editorSixteen segments become one seekable film.

A dedicated assembly pass joined sixteen chapter cuts, merged their captions, and wrote the timeline that drives navigation. The current film uses licensed stock-voice takes and remains open for creative review.

Story & production

A single meeting story, scripted and visualized for review.

11Creative directorOne Friday review carries the full film.

The treatment set a continuous meeting arc in which people make the decision and Dial Bot helps with evidence, actions, and catch-up. Product shots were directed to follow real Meetings UI.

12ScriptwriterDialogue with motives and clear handoffs.

The chapter script turned feature beats into a conversation and absorbed continuity feedback about why a person asks, where an answer goes, and what the rest of the room is doing.

13Storyboard artistScenes mapped to the actual meeting view.

The storyboard lays out participant cards, Host Controls, room and private chat, motion, and layout transitions so the film can be checked against Meetings rather than an invented interface.

14Casting directorPerformance guidance for the chapter cast.

The casting packet describes character purpose and performance, while retaining Dial Bot’s established voice direction. It is a planning artifact; final voice takes are still subject to review.

15Video producerThe first assembled scene is available to inspect.

The opening chapter shows the join transition, quiet Bot arrival, and private welcome in the meeting view.

16Audio engineerAn earlier voiced film with timed captions.

A separate production pass assembled 22 licensed stock-voice takes into a dialogue-only mix, four role stems, and timed captions for an earlier short film. It does not establish final voice casting for the 16-chapter demo.

17Video producerSeven staged scenes extend the meeting.

A production pass rendered the next seven chapter segments with the Chapter 1 meeting geometry and active-speaker cues. Licensed stock-voice takes now replace the early timing voices. The Jira and Glean examples are staged records rather than live connected results.

18Video producerEight more staged scenes complete the arc.

A second pass completed the later chapters, including Amit in a four-person grid. It kept the timer continuous and treated the proposed calendar booking as not booked. Licensed stock voices now replace the early timing takes; product actions still need real test-room capture.

19Visual designerSharper chat, card cues, and meeting controls.

Focused image reviews corrected chat text scale, the active speaker ring, and bottom control proportions in the Chapter 1 render. These files show the visual changes directly rather than claiming new product functionality.

20UX designerA private recap while the meeting continues.

An isolated experience study explored a late arrival reading a private recap without leaving the live conversation. The handoff labels the interaction as illustrative pending exact product UI verification.

Review & delivery

Product truth, coverage, QA, and a separate marketing exploration.

21Design QA specialistReal controls and cards guide the film.

A product reference checks participant cards, Host Controls, chat, timer, and bottom-bar placement against the actual Meetings interface and supplied captures.

22Product analystEach scene has an evidence boundary.

The coverage map compares the planned film with Yacine’s meeting work, distinguishing described behavior, reported live checks, and capture still needed for the finished demo.

23QA engineerFocused tests for the meeting path.

Post-merge local changes repaired Bot stop callback and canvas media tests. This credit covers the test work, not a claim that the full production flow has passed every check.

24QA specialistA repeatable way to inspect meeting behavior.

A local Virtual QA artifact prepared evidence-backed scenarios and a repeatable review path for Dial Bot meeting behavior, including clear separation of observed and proposed states.

25Web designerA separate preview for the existing site section.

The website exploration produced two private layouts for the current “In the flow of the meeting” section. The chosen layout now carries the chapter films within the existing marketing section.

26Market researcherClaims checked against the category.

A comparative research pass reviewed current meeting assistants and set boundaries for the page story: spoken participation, source-backed help, and honest limits. It explicitly avoided unsupported first-to-market and live-availability claims.

27Documentation designerA map of what is captured, previewed, or missing.

The screenshot-board assignment produced a three-lane manifest and self-contained SVG workflow for visual review. Its status fields distinguish captured product states from previews and missing evidence.
